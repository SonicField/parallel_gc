# Free-threaded lazy-pool benchmark analysis

## Result

Lazy helper creation removes the previous `sqlite_synth` regression. The rigorous ABBA result changed from 10.4 percent slower before lazy creation to 0.013 percent slower, which is not significant. Python startup is also neutral.

The change does not resolve every free-threaded performance problem. The project-specific sustained `ai_workload` remains 9.9 percent slower and its mean GC callback interval increases by 116 percent. Pyperformance's `float` benchmark is the only statistically significant whole-program regression, at 10.5 percent slower.

The parallel collector remains effective on substantial cyclic heaps. The project mixed workload gains 23.5 percent throughput, the requested 500,000-object collections improve by a 1.24 geometric mean, and the pyperformance GC-focused measurements improve by 16.8 to 41.8 percent.

## Provenance

The measurements used a Python 3.16 free-threaded PGO+LTO build on a 72-logical-CPU AArch64 Linux host with GCC 11.5.0. CPython commit `195830171f` contains the measured collector implementation. Commit `c67363fa59` changes only Python test guards for release builds and was the clean source-tree head when the campaign record was written. The parent repository was at `ed053b621c8cf0325d7b2d691099a679de4b3832` when measurement began.

The executable SHA-256 is `1fd1e93ae30e910016a5517e6533a3b5b10533abc1962d760c66cb52be5f5e7a`. It was configured with `--with-parallel-gc --disable-gil --enable-optimizations --with-lto`. Its supplementary PGO task reported that free-threaded parallel GC ran while training.

The implementation was verified before performance measurement:

- The free-threaded debug focused suite ran 368 tests with 39 expected skips.
- The same focused suite passed under AddressSanitizer with no sanitizer diagnostic.
- The lifecycle, fork, deque, and upstream free-threaded GC selection ran 155 tests under ThreadSanitizer with 25 expected skips and no sanitizer diagnostic.
- All 25 lifecycle tests passed against the optimized release build.
- The benchmark harness ran 15 unit tests successfully.

## Project-specific full benchmark

The full suite used five 60-second runs in each mode, 500,000 requested objects for collection measurements, and the adaptive controller for parallel arms.

| Experiment | Serial | Parallel | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed throughput | 2,753/s | 3,400/s | +23.5% |
| Mixed mean callback interval | 3.7 ms | 2.7 ms | -26% |
| Mixed maximum callback interval | 33 ms | 14 ms | -60% |
| Mixed time inside GC callbacks | 67.2% | 59.8% | -7.4 percentage points |
| 500,000-object collection geomean | — | — | 1.24 times faster |
| Sustained synthetic throughput geomean | — | — | +7.0% |
| Sustained synthetic callback-interval geomean | — | — | +18% |

Every requested 500,000-object collection shape improves. Individual improvements range from 1.12 times for `ai_workload` to 1.51 times for `layered`.

The sustained results are mixed:

| Shape | Throughput change | Mean callback change | Maximum callback change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Chain | +5.3% | +13% | 32.2 s to 42.0 s |
| Graph | +29.2% | -33% | 529 ms to 674 ms |
| AI workload | -9.9% | +116% | 3.38 s to 14.39 s |

The chain throughput samples range from 5.39 to 6.97 million/s serially and 5.44 to 7.27 million/s in parallel mode. The reported +5.3 percent mean therefore has high run-to-run dispersion. Its callback regression is more consistent and remains unresolved.

The `ai_workload` regression is the primary negative project result. Its maximum callback interval increases more than fourfold. Callback interval is the full time between GC start and stop callbacks; it is not a direct free-threaded stop-the-world measurement.

## Rigorous pyperformance ABBA

The campaign used disabled, enabled, enabled, disabled order, rigorous mode, CPU affinity `0-31`, and a 3,600-second per-benchmark timeout. Every result carries the expected parallel-GC mode attestation. It completed 93 benchmark programs producing 119 named measurements.

The geometric mean of enabled/disabled time across those 119 measurements is 0.97488, or 2.51 percent faster. This is not a representative application-level speedup: pyperformance gives equal weight to each named measurement, and the result includes 16 related `async_tree` variants. The per-benchmark results are the useful evidence.

There are 24 statistically significant improvements and one statistically significant regression. The largest GC-relevant changes are:

| Measurement | Disabled | Enabled | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `gc_traversal` | 1.574 ms | 0.916 ms | 41.8% faster |
| `create_gc_cycles` | 1.321 ms | 1.020 ms | 22.7% faster |
| `btree_gc_only` | 19.685 ms | 16.371 ms | 16.8% faster |
| `float` | 66.576 ms | 73.554 ms | 10.5% slower |

The result relevant to lazy creation is neutral:

| Measurement | Disabled | Enabled | Change | Significance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `sqlite_synth` | 1.308446 us | 1.308610 us | +0.013% | Not significant |
| `python_startup` | 16.084 ms | 16.114 ms | +0.187% | Not significant |
| `python_startup_no_site` | 7.458 ms | 7.449 ms | -0.121% | Not significant |

These results support the lazy-pool hypothesis: arming parallel GC without reaching an eligible collection no longer pays the permanent glibc first-thread transition. They do not show that active parallel collection is free; the project workload and GC-focused pyperformance measurements exercise that separate case.

Seven default pyperformance programs were excluded. `dask`, `fastapi`, `genshi`, and `xdsl` are incompatible with the pinned dependencies on Python 3.16 in this environment. The three `networkx` variants were not measured because this optimized executable lacks `_bz2`. That is a build-dependency coverage gap, not a collector result. The comparison therefore describes the 93 matched programs, not all 100 default programs.

Raw legs, combined files, the comparison table, executable hash, and repository states are stored under [`pyperformance/ft-lazy-full-2026-10-08`](pyperformance/ft-lazy-full-2026-10-08/).

## Remaining performance questions

The next investigations should target two independent negative regions:

1. Attribute the sustained free-threaded `ai_workload` throughput and callback-interval regression to a collection phase, with hardware counters and phase timing rather than a threshold change.
2. Reproduce and profile the pyperformance `float` regression. Its 10.5 percent effect appears in both enabled legs against both disabled legs and is not explained by eager thread creation.

The adaptive controller also needs longer convergence experiments. This campaign shows that it avoids poor fixed counts but does not reach fixed 16 within three cycles on this hardware.
